# Intelligence Lab

**A long-term research workspace for studying the computational principles of intelligence.**

This repository is the durable source of truth for the learning roadmap, research notes, paper reviews, experiments, and weekly research digests.

## Mission

Build a rigorous understanding of how intelligent systems learn, represent information, generalise, reason, remember, and adapt. The goal is not simply to learn how to use AI models, but to understand the mechanisms and principles that make them work.

## Research pillars

1. **Learning dynamics** — optimisation, generalisation, grokking, scaling, and emergence.
2. **Representations** — embeddings, feature learning, superposition, circuits, and internal representations.
3. **Memory and continual learning** — retention, retrieval, adaptation, catastrophic forgetting, and persistent memory.
4. **Computational foundations** — mathematics, probability, information theory, and neuroscience-inspired models.

## Learning principles

- Derive important ideas; do not rely only on memorised explanations.
- Implement key algorithms from first principles where practical.
- Read papers with explicit attention to claims, assumptions, methods, and limitations.
- Keep experiments reproducible and record negative results.
- Separate established findings, preprints, hypotheses, and speculation.
- Prefer depth of understanding over the number of topics completed.

## Suggested weekly rhythm

A sustainable starting allocation is six hours per week:

- 1 hour — mathematical foundations
- 1 hour — machine-learning theory
- 1 hour — paper reading
- 2 hours — implementation and experiments
- 1 hour — journal and review

Adjust this allocation as work commitments and research priorities change.
